## 1. Source skeleton

### Research provenance and chronology cautions

The [Gutenberg catalogue](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/70788) identifies the original publication as Charles Scribner's Sons, 1927. The [Internet Archive scan catalogue](https://archive.org/details/canarymurdercase00vand) also identifies publication in 1927. The Gutenberg transcriber note anomalously says 1926; this conflicts with both catalogue records and is not silently treated as authoritative. The scan catalogue was checked, not collated page by page. The [Standard Ebooks edition](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/s-s-van-dine/the-canary-murder-case) derives its transcription from Gutenberg and points to that scan, so it is not an independent witness for every textual claim.

Do not assign the plot the year 1927 merely from its publication date. The novel dates the first murder to Monday, September 10 and its solution to Tuesday, September 18, without this draft assigning a year. Gutenberg notes that it corrected chapter XXIX's inconsistent Tuesday, September 16 dateline to September 18. Times are often approximate recollections, not synchronized records. The timeline below preserves that limitation.

### Culprit, victim, motive, and actual first murder

The culprit is **Kenneth Spotswoode**, a married manufacturer of automobile accessories, temporarily staying at the Stuyvesant Club. The victim is **Margaret Odell**, called the Canary after a bird-ballet performance, a former Follies performer living at 184 West 71st Street, New York. Do not replace this with a film-derived characterization of her career. [Chapters I](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch01) and [XV](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch15).

In his final confession, Spotswoode says Odell demanded that he divorce his wife and marry her. When he refused, she threatened disclosure to his wife, copies of his letters, legal proceedings, and scandal. He feared losing his wife and children. His business had been in receivership for almost a year and his apparently grand estate belonged to his wife; his wealthy-public-man persona concealed approaching bankruptcy. These are the killer's account and rationalization, not a moral endorsement of killing or a neutral description of the victim as deserving her fate. [Chapter XXX](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch30).

After returning from dinner and theatre, he manually strangles her from behind while she sits on the davenport. He retrieves his letters and stages burglary with gloves: disturbed furniture, broken mirror, torn dress, missing jewelry, ransacked drawers, and an amateur attempt to force a steel jewel case with a poker. The medical examination describes neck-pressure marks and a struggle. Vance reconstructs use of the victim's key to open the document box; confession confirms letter retrieval, but not every reconstructed intermediate act. [Chapters IV](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch04), [XIV](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch14), [XXVI](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch26), and [XXX](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch30).

### Fabricated proof of life and genuine alibi

The apparatus is a wind-up phonograph with a specially prepared twelve-inch disc: initially silent grooves, then screams and cries for help, a pause for a prepared question, then a female-sounding reassurance. **The recorded voice is the killer's falsetto, not a recording of the living victim.** He disguises the disc with a Beethoven symphony label and brings it among other records. After the murder/staging, he starts playback, leaves the apartment, and asks switchboard operator Jessup to call a taxi. He watches elapsed time while standing with Jessup. When recorded screams sound about five minutes later, he runs to the closed door with Jessup, knocks, and asks at the intended pause; the fixed answer appears responsive. The phonograph's automatic stop ends playback. A thin door and the horn's position close to it help disguise recorded sound. [Chapters VI](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch06) and [XXIX](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch29).

Jessup never sees a living Odell during this apparent exchange. Spotswoode departs in a taxi and is independently seen by Judge Redfern at the club at about 11:50 p.m.; the later poker gathering lasts until about 3 a.m. That alibi is genuine for the later interval. Its force depends on the mistaken belief that Odell was alive when the voice replied. The source's five-minute disc duration is Vance's estimate, not a published laboratory measurement. [Chapters IX](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch09) and [XXIX](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch29).

### Hidden witness, independent entry trick, and second murder

**Tony "Dude" Skeel**, Odell's former lover and a professional burglar, has an apartment key. At around 9:30 he visibly makes an unsuccessful call, but while outside the operator's view unbolts the building's rear/side entrance. He visibly leaves by the front, circles around, and returns through that side door. When Odell and Spotswoode return around eleven, he hides in the living-room clothes closet, moves its normally external key inside, and locks himself in. The keyhole faces the davenport. The killer does not know he is there. Inner closet-knob prints support concealment; confession confirms the witnessed killing. [Chapters VII](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch07), [XII](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch12), [XXV](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch25), and [XXX](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch30).

After Spotswoode leaves, Skeel emerges. Vance reconstructs the recorded screams startling him beside the already-dead woman, causing the fresh handprint on the table; that emotional sequence is an inference. Skeel answers Cleaver's approximately 11:40 phone call, later weeps where Cleaver can overhear, professionally chisels open the jewel case, and takes a solitaire ring left inside. Theft and murder are separate actions by different people. [Chapters XIV](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch14), [XXI](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch21), and [XXIX](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch29).

Skeel's exit also explains the supposedly impossible bolted side door. A small eyebrow-tweezers/purple-twine arrangement lets him rotate the turn-bolt from outside and retrieve the apparatus under the door. Vance later demonstrates this with the recovered objects. Two observations of a bolted door do not establish it was bolted all night. **This is Skeel's independent escape mechanism; Spotswoode leaves normally through the front. Skeel is not an accomplice and does not create the phonograph alibi.** [Chapter XXV](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch25).

On Tuesday night, September 11, Skeel phones Spotswoode, mentions the phonograph, meets him at the Waldorf, and demands a large payment. Spotswoode pays a small amount and asks for time to realize assets. On Saturday Skeel threatens to tell Markham everything at a Sunday 10 a.m. appointment. Spotswoode goes to Skeel's rooms for a promised money delivery Saturday night, strangles him, locks the room, takes the key, and leaves openly. The disclosure deadline motivates silencing. [Chapters XXII–XXIV](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch22) and [XXX](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch30).

### How Vance reaches the solution — and its limits

Vance first questions staged-scene anomalies and distinguishes amateur damage from professional theft. Prints and the closet suggest a concealed occupant. He tests misleading alibis and obtains fuller testimony. Skeel's second death suggests blackmail and one killer behind both crimes. The recovered tweezers/twine explain the bolted door and remove a supposedly exclusive means attributed to Jessup. These are productive material reasoning steps. [Chapters XIV](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch14), [XXIV](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch24), and [XXV](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch25).

He then arranges poker hands using card manipulator Wiley Allen and interprets the suspects' risk-taking. Cleaver bets on near certainty; Mannix's calculated/doubled chances seem less extreme; Spotswoode bluffs a worthless hand against a strong opponent by reading the person. Vance identifies that daring temperament with the murders and names Spotswoode. **This does not logically eliminate innocent alternatives.** Markham does not accept it as arrest evidence, and Vance still cannot explain the voice alibi. [Chapters XXVII](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch27) and [XXVIII](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch28).

At the apartment, Vance notices record packaging, opens the machine, and tries the purported classical record as music. After silent playback and a needle change, the screams and reassurance expose the trick. This is a materially decisive discovery but occurs accidentally rather than through a complete earlier deduction. Confession then supplies motive, record preparation, attempted retrieval, blackmail, and second killing. [Chapters XXIX](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch29) and [XXX](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70788/pg70788-images.html#ch30).

Therefore the useful structural insight is **false survival invalidates the inferred death interval while preserving a real alibi**. It is not that psychology uniquely proves guilt. A fair interactive adaptation should make the false-life evidence deliberately discoverable and independently attribute the staging before requiring a final accusation. The source's assumptions about character, ancestry, appearance, and mental illness remain period attitudes, not current factual principles.

## 2. Adaptation principles

**PROPOSED:** retain a genuine, strongly corroborated alibi for the wrong period. The killer really is elsewhere throughout the supposed death window. The player changes that window by disproving a supposed living contact. Retain competing motives, authentic recorded speech mistaken for present consciousness, a concealed eyewitness with a separate wrongdoing, blackmail, a second killing, and old evidence gaining new meaning.

Replace the cast, relationships, setting, dialogue, objects, financial conflict, proof-of-life machinery, and resolution. Replace psychological suspect sorting and accidental solution discovery with observations the player can deliberately investigate. The intended structure is **midpoint suspicion / identification → late proof**: Arman may be effectively established as Kian's killer at midpoint, but the first murder still needs an independent reconstruction. A confession confirms a solved chain; it supplies no essential fact.

The setting is a Persian-language creative community in **Mehrava**, a fictional city with **Narin House**, a residential building, and **Studio Eight**, a production venue. These names establish no real Tehran geography. **Roya Mehr**, 26, is a singer and online performer preparing an independent release. Give her ordinary work, friendship, ambition, and artistic agency. Her death is not punishment for visibility, relationships, or being a woman who performs.

The principal motive is impending financial exposure and loss of career control. Roya's emailed spreadsheet shows discrepancies, but her physical packet contains the immediately available signed corroboration tying Arman personally to deliberate payment redirection. He goes to pressure her and recover those originals, not to execute a fully engineered murder. When she refuses, the confrontation escalates. Only afterward does he combine the booked delivery, familiar away greeting, and imminent showcase into a false-timeline plan. Roya seeks an accountant review, not silence money. Kian separately attempts blackmail after witnessing the killing.

Avoid privileged intuition treated as proof, personality or appearance treated as criminal identification, exact postmortem timing claims, sexual shame as a stock motive, and physical strength as a suspect filter. A voice, message, connection indicator, or delivery status does not automatically prove a living person. Timestamps need provenance; camera coverage has boundaries; credentials may be shared.

Cultural rewriting must cover performer income, family relationships, dialogue register, privacy, Kian's choices, and access to sensitive material. Use fictional institutional arrangements pending a Persian-language cultural reader. Do not invent claims about Iranian law, police powers, or restrictions on performance. The proposed player assists a small authorized investigation team; the exact professional role remains open.

## 4. Canonical truth

**PROPOSED, author knowledge:** Arman diverted sponsor funds through a vendor he controls. Roya emailed Laleh a spreadsheet of incoming totals, expected payments, and shortfalls. It flags discrepancies but excludes the **signed payment-redirection instructions and accompanying side letter** in her blue packet. Those originals link Arman's personal directions to money withheld from Roya, undermining explanations of clerical delay or vendor error. Roya described them to Laleh and intends to bring them at 09:30 next morning.

Arman knows about the review and thinks recovering originals will remove the clearest immediately available proof and let him dispute the spreadsheet. His reasoning is self-serving and imperfect. He does not know that counterparties' ordinary retained authorization correspondence and payment records can later corroborate the directions independently. Killing cannot erase bank history or an accountant's suspicions. His motive is suppression of a damaging witness and signed corroboration under pressure, not a guarantee that one packet eliminates all exposure.

He visits at 20:43 intending to pressure Roya into surrendering originals and postponing review. She refuses, insists on the deadline, and rejects his explanation.
